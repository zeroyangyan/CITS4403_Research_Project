# CITS4403 Research Project

## The Impact of Network Structure and Fact-Checking Strategies on Misinformation Spread

A simulation-based investigation of how social-network topology and fact-checker placement affect misinformation diffusion. The project uses a synchronous, stochastic agent-based model (ABM) and compares **Erdős–Rényi random**, **Watts–Strogatz small-world**, and **Barabási–Albert scale-free** networks.

### Research questions

1. How do network structures and fact-checking strategies affect misinformation reach and speed?
2. With a fixed number of fact-checkers, does placement on high-degree nodes outperform random placement?

## Model

Each vertex represents an agent and each undirected edge represents an interaction channel. Agents have one of four mutually exclusive states:

- **Unaware** — has not adopted misinformation.
- **Believer** — currently believes and can pass on misinformation.
- **Corrected** — no longer believes and, when enabled, can transmit corrections.
- **Resistant** — cannot adopt misinformation.

Fixed fact-checkers begin Corrected and do not become Believers. Each time step, a Believer may expose its Unaware neighbours, while eligible correction sources may expose Believers to corrective information. Transitions are evaluated from the old state and applied **synchronously**. With `k` exposures and per-exposure probability `p`, the transition probability is `1 - (1 - p)**k`. Agents do not spontaneously forget misinformation or relapse after correction in this model.

Three intervention types are compared: `none`, `random` (randomly selected fact-checkers), and `centrality` (highest-degree nodes). `centrality` here specifically means **degree-based** targeting, not betweenness or PageRank.

### Behavioural heterogeneity

The main experiment represented by `results/combined_final.csv` compares `homogeneous`, `low`, and `high` levels. The `low` and `high` settings use fixed agent probabilities generated by a **paired symmetric uniform-deviation** procedure, rather than Beta sampling; see [docs/HETEROGENEITY.md](docs/HETEROGENEITY.md). An additional Beta-based `model_mode` implementation is also present in the repository, but the uploaded source has unresolved merge conflicts in the combined logic, so it should not be assumed to reproduce the archived CSV without repair.

## Experiments and archived results

The uploaded `results/combined_final.csv` contains **810 simulation records**, structured as:

- 3 network structures × 3 intervention strategies × 3 heterogeneity levels × 30 repeats = **810 runs**;
- `n = 500`, nominal mean degree `8`, `5` initial Believers, maximum `150` time steps;
- single-exposure misinformation adoption probability `0.15` and correction probability `0.35`;
- resistant fraction `0.05`; intervention fraction `0.05` (25 fact-checkers at 500 nodes), except no-intervention conditions.

All 27 network–heterogeneity–intervention cells have 30 recorded repetitions. **This file does not document 31,500 runs.** If a larger experiment exists outside the supplied archive, it must be added and separately documented before claiming that total.

### Key outcome: final misinformation reach

Mean `final_reach` for the **homogeneous** portion of `results/combined_final.csv` (30 repetitions per cell):

| Network | No intervention | Random fact-checking | Degree-targeted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| random | 0.950 | 0.712 | 0.616 |
| small_world | 0.950 | 0.691 | 0.606 |
| scale_free | 0.950 | 0.549 | 0.175 |

`final_reach` is the fraction of agents that have ever become Believers by the end of the simulation; it is **not** the fraction of active Believers at the final time step. Lower values indicate better containment. In these archived runs, degree-targeted intervention has lower mean final reach than random selection in all three tested networks; its advantage is particularly large in the scale-free network. These are results of the specified stylised model, not a causal estimate for real social networks.

Other fields include `peak_believers`, `peak_believer_fraction`, `time_to_peak`, `extinction_time`, and `ended_with_active_believers`. Interpret `time_to_peak` cautiously when prevalence plateaus, and treat extinction times as unavailable when no extinction occurred.

The `results/figures/` folder contains earlier 9-condition summaries and plots; these should **not** be presented as summaries of all 810 runs without regeneration. See [results/README.md](results/README.md).

## Assumptions and limitations

- Networks are stylised, static, and undirected; no empirical user network is fitted.
- Each run uses fixed response characteristics; no behavioural learning, relapse, forgetting, fatigue, or delayed intervention.
- A high-degree node is treated as an effective target based on topology, not measured influence.
- At baseline, unchecked misinformation can persist because Believers do not spontaneously stop spreading.
- Intervention strategies share network realisations in the combined experiment, while resistant users and initial Believers may be sampled independently across strategies. Comparisons are not fully identical-initial-condition experiments.
- Distributional assumptions for heterogeneity are hypothetical and have not been empirically validated.
- The archived results do not by themselves establish that the currently bundled code can reproduce them until merge issues are fixed.
